# Free, Publicly Shared Retouching Techniques of Elite Beauty/Fashion Retouchers (Fejes, McCullough, Dresser, Woloszynowicz, PHLEARN, Calvin Hollywood, Valentine, Carney, Sherk, and others)

Scope note: this covers free, legitimately accessible material only (free blog articles, KelbyOne Insider, Fstoppers, PHLEARN free tutorial pages, Retouching Academy free articles, podcast show notes, free interview pages). Several primary sources could not be fetched (YouTube pages hit a captcha; PopPhoto, DPReview, and viktorfejes.com returned 403; vibrantshot.com returned 503; CreativeLive lesson pages redirect to the paywalled class page). Those gaps are listed per question. Research date: 2026-10-02.

## Q1. Viktor Fejes: signature D&B method, skin texture approach, free content, interviews

### Takeaway
Fejes's free material is mostly about how he thinks, not exact settings. In it he says retouching is a "thought process" and that a retoucher should be "invisible". His published course outlines show his method in structure: retouch with Curves adjustment layers only, check with "black and white visual aid" helper layers and "false colors", and handle texture, global tones, local tones and color as separate, ordered stages. No free source gives his exact curve values, brush settings or a named "Fejes D&B" method.

### Cited Findings
- Fejes is a high-end advertising and editorial retoucher who has worked from Shanghai to Los Angeles, specializing in portrait, beauty and fashion retouching — [CreativeLive class page](https://www.creativelive.com/class/retouch-with-curves-in-photoshop-viktor-fejes)
- His CreativeLive course "Retouch with Curves in Photoshop" covers "the best use of curves, how saturation and hue work, and dodging and burning with curves". Its lessons include setting up with curves, spot cleanup, "How to use Black and White Visual Aids", global dodge and burn, local dodge and burn, and local color correction. The course says you can retouch an entire image using only Curves adjustment layers. Only the lesson titles and the free intro are public. — [CreativeLive](https://www.creativelive.com/class/retouch-with-curves-in-photoshop-viktor-fejes); [lesson: How to use B&W Visual Aids](https://www.creativelive.com/class/retouch-with-curves-in-photoshop-viktor-fejes/lessons/how-to-use-black-and-white-visual-aids); [lesson: Local Dodge and Burn](https://www.creativelive.com/class/retouch-with-curves-in-photoshop-viktor-fejes/lessons/how-to-do-local-dodge-and-burn); [lesson: Local Color Correction](https://www.creativelive.com/class/retouch-with-curves-in-photoshop-viktor-fejes/lessons/how-to-do-local-color-correction)
- His KelbyOne class "The Essential Elements of Retouching" (outline published free) runs in this order: Where to Begin → How to Think About Retouching → How an Image is Built → Raw Conversion → Structure Your Layers → False Colors → Destructive Editing → Texture → Tones: Global Adjustments → Tones: Local Adjustments → Color → Finishing Up. The class description says he treats retouching "in a logical and methodical fashion", uses false color to check texture, tones and color relationships, and treats global and local tonal work as separate passes. — [KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/its-new-class-thursday-the-essential-elements-of-retouching-with-viktor-fejes/)
- Free YouTube trailers or intros exist for "The Essential Elements of Retouching with Viktor Fejes" and "The Best Retouching Secrets: Uncovered with Viktor Fejes". Their content could not be fetched (captcha). — [YouTube WpJYaUY-38g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WpJYaUY-38g); [YouTube NiU5xEWUvPg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NiU5xEWUvPg); [KelbyOne Insider "Best Retouching Secrets"](https://insider.kelbyone.com/new-class-alert-the-best-retouching-secrets-uncovered-with-viktor-fejes/)
- Interview quotes: "Like photography, retouching isn't about the technology — it's a thought process. It's an extension of photography and, as a retoucher, you extend the story to enhance the focus on the most important parts." / "As a good retoucher, you want to be invisible." / "Retouching has become a lot more mild, it's far more human now." — [Visual Education interview](https://visualeducation.com/viktor-fejes-life-as-a-professional-retouch-artist/)
- In his Visual Education live sessions he retouches subscriber images, covering skin retouching, burning and dodging, healing and color correction. He says he always uses "a very, very soft brush" for D&B, and frames D&B as controlling shadows and highlights to "enhance three dimensionality". Some of this is from search snippets of the members' pages. — [Visual Education: Live Retouching with Viktor Fejes](https://visualeducation.com/class/live-retouching-with-viktor-fejes/); [Visual Education talk show](https://visualeducation.com/class/live-talk-show-with-special-guest-viktor-fejes/)
- Fejes is a Tripodcast co-host and the creative director of Gild Studios. He also has a Patreon and a Facebook page (@viktorfejesretoucher); their free posts were not retrievable. — [Visual Education interview](https://visualeducation.com/viktor-fejes-life-as-a-professional-retouch-artist/); [KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/its-new-class-thursday-the-essential-elements-of-retouching-with-viktor-fejes/); [Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/viktorfejes)

### Inferences
- The publicly visible "Fejes method" is: Curves-only and non-destructive, with a helper "perceptual B&W" layer for luminance D&B, then a separate hue and saturation pass ("how saturation and hue work"), all in a fixed order: raw → layer structure → texture (destructive cleanup) → global tones → local tones → color → finish. For an agent, a faithful reproduction is a pair of D&B Curves layers (global and local) checked under a B&W helper, followed by Curves-based local color correction on separate layers.
- "False colors" probably means a helper layer that maps luminance or saturation to exaggerated colors so uneven areas are easy to see. Treat this as unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No free source gives Fejes's exact curve shapes, brush flow or opacity, helper-layer construction, or a named "color-preserving D&B" method. The YouTube intros, Tripodcast episodes and Patreon free posts could not be fetched. viktorfejes.com returned 403.

## Q2. Sef McCullough: skin retouching and D&B approach, free tutorials

### Takeaway
McCullough's free material (Retouchist interview, Lensrentals podcast) is about workflow structure, not settings. He takes images apart into isolated elements early, uses "Tiered Clipping/Masking", treats frequency separation as a structure rather than a single tool, and keeps skin work "dialed back" for commercial clients. His D&B specifics sit in paid PRO EDU courses (which include a "Dodge & Burn Action").

### Cited Findings
- Commercial images "are often completely deconstructed, even the ones that appear as if they were never taken apart". He also says: "if there's more than a really general grade and cleanup, we'll just start breaking it down immediately" (isolating every element). — [Retouchist interview](http://retouchist.net/blog-1/2016/9/11/interview-with-pro-commercial-retoucher-sef-mccullough)
- He names "Tiered Clipping/Masking" as fundamental to modern commercial workflow and says it is rarely taught. — [Retouchist interview](http://retouchist.net/blog-1/2016/9/11/interview-with-pro-commercial-retoucher-sef-mccullough)
- For commercial (e.g., sports) skin, beauty technique is "usually dialed back a bit". — [Retouchist interview](http://retouchist.net/blog-1/2016/9/11/interview-with-pro-commercial-retoucher-sef-mccullough)
- Retouchers are "much less like photographers than we are painters once we're in post". He recommends figure drawing for anatomy, applied color theory, and the book *Color Choices* by Jeanne Armentrout. He calls the "wizard myth" around retouching "bullshit". — [Retouchist interview](http://retouchist.net/blog-1/2016/9/11/interview-with-pro-commercial-retoucher-sef-mccullough)
- In the podcast he describes frequency separation as splitting the image into a tone layer and a texture layer, "a structure vs a specific tool", which makes edits "more flexible and less painful". He also says "A lot of the job is just knowing the fundamentals and having the eye to see what needs to get fixed" and stresses "deep looking" (around 12:00). — [Lensrentals Podcast #27 show notes](https://www.lensrentals.com/blog/2020/09/the-lensrentals-podcast-episode-27-making-retouching-more-accessible-with-sef-mccullough/); [YouTube version](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kVc_B3WJzrM)
- His PRO EDU courses (paid) include "The Dodge & Burn Tool" lesson and a downloadable Dodge and Burn Action. PRO EDU also has a free YouTube Q&A playlist, "Sef McCullough Answers Questions About Commercial Retouching" (contents not fetched). — [PRO EDU](https://proedu.com/products/essential-photoshop-tools-sef-mccullough-pro-edu); [PRO EDU advanced](https://proedu.com/products/commercial-professional-photoshop-advanced-retouching-workflow-tutorial-with-sef-mccullough); [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLmCypeFRC4z4ogdZtRIWqMemlIvqpiTr0)

### Inferences
- For an agent, "McCullough-style" means a layer architecture more than a skin recipe. First isolate subject, skin, clothing and background with masks. Then clip groups of corrections to those masks in tiers (a parent mask group, with clipped D&B and color layers under each element). Keep frequency separation as an optional structural split, not the main smoothing tool.

### Gaps
- No free source gives McCullough's D&B settings, curve values, or the exact layout of "Tiered Clipping/Masking". The YouTube Q&A playlist could not be read.

## Q3. Amy Dresser: sculpting with light, celebrity/editorial approach, interviews

### Takeaway
Free sources agree that Dresser's work is mostly tonal sculpting (dodge and burn), plus Curves and channel-based color exploration. Her stated aim is to "keep the character, but remove what might be distracting", and she favors light-and-shadow illusions over reshaping bodies. Her detailed free demo (a 2.5-hour RetouchPRO face-sculpting video) is video-only. One claim about her selection technique (jagged, heavily feathered lasso selection plus a Curves layer) appears only in a search snippet and could not be traced to a primary source.

### Cited Findings
- Her stated aim: "keep the character, but remove what might be distracting". — [PhotoShelter Luminance speaker page](https://www.photoshelter.com/luminance/videos/dresser)
- She works by removing what is distracting, then adjusting channels up and down "to see if things will look more interesting with a different color direction". This is the interview summary's paraphrase, not a direct quote. — [Brian Smith summary of the PhotoShelter interview](https://briansmith.com/retoucher-amy-dresser-photoshelter-interview/)
- A free 2.5-hour start-to-finish portrait demo from RetouchPRO (YouTube) shows "tonal sculpting to enhance, minimize, or even create facial features". There is no text transcript. — [ISO 1200 Magazine](https://www.iso1200.com/2012/10/portrait-retouch-face-sculpting-with.html)
- She "spends 80% of her time dodging and burning", uses Curves for color, and does not use plug-ins. The source is a secondary review citing "one interview"; the search snippet attributes it to a 2012 PopPhoto "I, Photographer: Retouch Artist" piece, which returned 403. — [FixThePhoto review (secondary)](https://fixthephoto.com/amy-dresser-retouching-review.html); [PopPhoto (not fetched)](https://www.popphoto.com/how-to/2012/01/i-photographer-retouch-artist/)
- Her 2012 Luminance talk covered retouching's roles, from basic "dusting" to a complete environmental overhaul, her working relationships with photographers, and the "basic principles of retouching". — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/video/retoucher-amy-dresser-speaks-about-various-intentions-retouching-4478)
- Unverified: she uses shadows, highlights and even skin tone to create illusions instead of liquify-style "liposuction". She also makes a very jagged lasso selection with a large feather, well inside the target area, then adds a Curves layer through it. This appeared only in a WebSearch synthesis attributed to PopPhoto. FixThePhoto has neither claim, and the primary page could not be fetched. Treat both as unconfirmed. — [PopPhoto (not fetched)](https://www.popphoto.com/how-to/2012/01/i-photographer-retouch-artist/)

### Inferences
- An agent version of "Dresser sculpting": large-scale D&B with Curves through very soft (heavily feathered) masks, keeping each correction well inside the feature boundary, followed by small channel moves in Curves to test color directions. This matches the confirmed points (most time on D&B, Curves for color, channel adjustment).

### Gaps
- No verbatim technical quotes with settings. The DPReview thread "How does Amy Dresser do it?" (403) and the PopPhoto interview (403) could not be read. The lasso, feather and Curves recipe is unconfirmed.

## Q4. Michael Woloszynowicz (Vibrant Shot): free D&B and workflow details

### Takeaway
Woloszynowicz has published the most exact numbers of anyone in this group for free. His Capture One D&B article gives input/output curve values for corrective, strong-corrective and contouring layers, plus a helper layer. The Capture One blog gives his full four-stage workflow and its order in Photoshop.

### Cited Findings
- D&B in Capture One, with exact values (free Vibrant Shot blog post): [store.vibrantshot.com](https://store.vibrantshot.com/blogs/news/dodge-and-burn-with-capture-one-pro)

  | Layer | Settings |
  |---|---|
  | Corrective Dodge | RGB curve Input 124 → Output 146 |
  | Corrective Burn | RGB curve Input 146 → Output 124 |
  | Strong Corrective Dodge | RGB 137 → 153, plus Luma curve 141 → 174 |
  | Strong Corrective Burn | RGB 137 → 121, plus Luma curve 142 → 110 |
  | Contouring Dodge and Burn | Standard dodge and burn settings, layer opacity lowered to "50 or 60%" |
  | Helper layer | Saturation −100, Exposure −0.1, RGB curve 146 → 115, HDR Shadow 15, Clarity 5 |
  | Brush | Flow 5%, Opacity 100%, Hardness 0 |

  The basic setup is 3 layers (corrective dodge, corrective burn, helper); the extended setup is up to 7.
- His four-stage workflow: (1) tethered capture in Capture One; (2) raw processing; (3) skin retouching in Photoshop; (4) output via PSD roundtrip and variants in Capture One. — [Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/portrait-and-beauty-retouching-workflow)
- Photoshop order: "Perform all your raster/pixel based adjustments at the outset on separate layers with all adjustment layers placed above those." Then: (1) cleanup with Healing Brush or Clone Stamp (blemishes, stray hairs, sensor spots); (2) subject extraction (Quick Select plus Refine Edge) if useful; (3) corrective D&B with Curves adjustment layers and masks; (4) contouring D&B; (5) color correction with Hue/Saturation and Selective Color layers through feathered masks for mismatched skin tones; (6) color grading. — [Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/portrait-and-beauty-retouching-workflow)
- Raw-stage rules: use shadow and highlight sliders "sparingly" (fix the lighting instead). Avoid the contrast slider and use Levels and Curves, pulling the black and white points inward. For moody contrast, "drive down the center point of the curve" and pull the lower-right Levels handle in until the histogram stretches again. Use the Skin Tone tab's Uniformity sliders, but saturation uniformity only "sparingly" so skin doesn't go flat, and don't pull lip or clothing color into it. Keep Clarity (Natural) "under 6 or 7" for portraits. Color Balance: "apply complementary colors between" highlights and shadows. Export 16-bit for editorial or commercial work. — [Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/portrait-and-beauty-retouching-workflow)
- Finishing: slight vignette; Natural Clarity at "1-4" for midtone contrast; film grain (more for B&W than color); judge sharpening at 100% with recipe proofing. — [Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/portrait-and-beauty-retouching-workflow)
- His free 30-minute video compares four D&B approaches: layer blend modes, Curves adjustments, frequency separation, and Blend If. The article text gives no settings. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2014/12/21/4-techniques-will-help-master-dodging-burning-photos/)
- His paid course (Retouching Academy or Fstoppers "Master Dodge & Burn", 3.5 hours) covers the theory of hue, saturation and luminosity, and localized correction alongside the Healing Brush. — [Retouching Academy](https://retouchingacademy.com/product/master-dodge-burn-video-course/)

### Inferences
- In 8-bit terms, the corrective curves shift the midtones by about ±22 levels (124↔146), a gentle curve. Painted at 5% flow, each stroke changes the image very little. Photoshop equivalent: Dodge = Curves (124,146), Burn = Curves (146,124), with black masks and a soft brush (Flow 5%, Opacity 100%, Hardness 0). The helper layer is desaturated, slightly darkened, and low-contrast in the shadows (HDR shadow +15), so midtone transitions are easier to see.

### Gaps
- vibrantshot.com's blog index (503) could not be fetched for other free text articles. The settings in the four-approaches video are not transcribed.

## Q5. Scott Valentine, Lisa Carney, Kristina Sherk: specific free techniques

### Takeaway
Valentine's free KelbyOne Insider articles give exact recipes for helper layers (Solar curve, Mid-Peak, Luminosity/Negative), for D&B with a Curves pair split into large, medium and small passes, and for synthetic skin texture. Sherk's free "Iris Pop" eye method is fully specified (Color Dodge Hue/Saturation, Blend If 87/109–152/168). Carney's free material is mostly high-level (spot → frequency separation → enhance); her detailed color tricks (evening skin with Hue, Selective Color, Gradient Map, Match Color with Curves) are in paid CreativeLive lessons, apart from free lesson previews.

### Cited Findings
**Scott Valentine: helper layers ([KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/using-helper-layers-when-retouching-your-photos-by-scott-valentine/))**
- "Solar" (solarization curve): "After adding a Curves adjustment layer... click about 1/4 of the way up on the line, and drag up, then click another 1/4 to the right and drag down, then repeat so you have a total of four points on the curve." Use it to exaggerate blemishes, check texture during healing and cloning, and reveal D&B unevenness.
- "Mid Peak": a Curves layer. "Click on the middle of the curve and drag it to the top. Now click on the highlights anchor point in the top right and drag it straight down. Finally, move the white control slider... slightly in to the left, and the black control slider... a bit in to the right." This shows skin texture and gradient smoothness better than Solar. Use it with the Spot Healing Brush on separate layers set to Darken or Lighten.
- Luminosity and Negative: a Solid Color fill layer in Color mode with S = 0%, B = 50%, plus a Curves layer inverted (left anchor to top, right anchor to bottom). Group both with the D&B layers and "Toggle the group on/off every few strokes".

**Scott Valentine: D&B ([Peachpit excerpt](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=3104540))**
- Dodge: "drag the center of the curve upwards to the point where the darkest parts of your image are as light as you think you'd ever want them to be. Name this layer Dodge. Fill the mask with black." Burn: "drag its curve down so that the brightest areas are as dark as you want them to be." Paint white on the masks with "a soft brush with low settings for Flow and Opacity".
- "Using one pair of Curves each for large, medium, and small features gives you a lot of flexibility." Order: large shaping and visual focus → intermediate features → detail pass.
- He prefers the Curves pair over the Overlay 50%-gray painting method because it limits artifacts and extreme changes, and keeps lightening and darkening on separate layers. — [KelbyOne Insider search summary](https://insider.kelbyone.com/using-helper-layers-when-retouching-your-photos-by-scott-valentine/)

**Scott Valentine: color matching and skin ([KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/color-matching-with-helper-layers-by-scott-valentine/))**
- Skin tone is fixed last: "as viewers, we're more forgiving of 'correct' skin tones regardless of surrounding colors." After a global Selective Color match, he uses a "large, soft, round brush, set the Opacity and Flow to 30%... black... lightly dab her face and hands on the Selective Color adjustment layer's mask." This lets some original skin color back in for a natural look.

**Scott Valentine: synthetic skin texture ([KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/synthetic-skin-part-2-by-scott-valentine/))**
- Start with a 4000×4000 px, 8-bit, 50% gray document. Apply Add Noise "50%, Gaussian, Monochromatic" → Crystallize 3 → Gaussian Blur 1.5–4 px → Emboss "90°, 6 px, and 50%". Save as PSD. Apply it with the Texturizer filter on a gray-filled layer set to Vivid Light, "Fill at 70%, Opacity at 80%". Mask it more strongly in darker areas, to mimic less visible texture where light is indirect. Shine layer: brush at 50% Opacity, 30% Flow, Linear Dodge mode, Fill 35%.

**Kristina Sherk: "Iris Pop" ([KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/adding-punch-contrast-to-eyes-by-kristina-sherk/))**
- Hue/Saturation adjustment layer named "Iris Pop", blend mode Color Dodge, Saturation +52. Layer Style → Blend If (Underlying Layer): start at 101/133, then Alt-split to "87/109 and 152/168" to soften the edges and reveal iris striations. Invert the mask (Cmd/Ctrl-I). Paint white at 30% with a soft round brush "on the opposite side of the iris from where the catchlight is", e.g., "the lower-right third of each iris". Reduce layer opacity if needed. Works best "on eyes that have native contrast in them to begin with."
- Her free-preview CreativeLive and KelbyOne classes cover under-eyes, opening eyes, whitening, lashes, catchlights, and a teeth-whitening action; their settings are not in free text. — [KelbyOne Insider](https://insider.kelbyone.com/its-new-class-thursday-electrifying-eyes-retouching-eyes-in-photoshop-with-kristina-sherk/); [CreativeLive teeth lesson](https://www.creativelive.com/class/incorporating-actions-into-your-portraits-workflow-kristina-sherk/lessons/teeth-whitening-action)

**Lisa Carney**
- Quick portrait workflow (free Rangefinder article and video): (1) spotting and distraction removal with Heal, Spot Heal and Clone, plus Content-Aware Fill for crosshairs (~5 min); (2) skin and clothing smoothing with frequency separation, "separating the texture in the image from the color and tone" (~10 min); (3) enhancements: add eyelash detail, lighten eyes, highlight facial features. She also advises "pre-touching", reviewing shots during the shoot to fix problems in camera. A free fabric-retouching PDF is linked. — [lisacarney.com/rangefinder-magazine](https://www.lisacarney.com/rangefinder-magazine)
- Her CreativeLive "Color Techniques for Retouching" lessons (some free previews) include Curves adjustment layers, Selective Color, "Even Skin Tone: Hue", Gradient Map, Match Color, and changing hair color. The description says she teaches "evening" skin tone rather than changing it. — [CreativeLive class](https://www.creativelive.com/class/color-techniques-for-retouching-lisa-carney); [Even Skin Tone: Hue](https://www.creativelive.com/class/color-techniques-for-retouching-lisa-carney/lessons/even-skin-tone-hue); [Match Color lesson](https://www.creativelive.com/class/color-techniques-for-retouching-lisa-carney/lessons/match-color-image-adjustments)

### Inferences
- The Valentine helper stack (Solar + Mid Peak + 50%-gray Color + inverted Curves) is fully specified, so an agent can build it programmatically and use it as a QA view for D&B uniformity.
- Sherk's Iris Pop translates directly into code: a Color Dodge saturation boost limited by luminance (Blend If 87/109–152/168 on the underlying layer), masked to the third of the iris opposite the catchlight.

### Gaps
- Carney's exact settings for "Even Skin Tone: Hue", Match Color and Gradient Map are behind CreativeLive (lesson pages redirect to the paid class). No free Sherk teeth settings were found; generic yellow-desaturation methods are not hers.

## Q6. PHLEARN (Aaron Nace) free tutorials: skin, hair, color settings

### Takeaway
PHLEARN's free tutorial pages give exact settings for frequency separation (8-bit and 16-bit Apply Image), hair work (Liquify Pressure and Density 25, Clone Stamp at Darken or Lighten with Current & Below at ~85% hardness), and a texture-preserving smoothing method (textured clone brush at 20–30% flow, then a clipped, desaturated High Pass layer in Overlay).

### Cited Findings
- Frequency separation: blur the low layer until "pores and fine lines have just vanished but the shape of the face... is still clearly readable", typically 4–10 px on standard portraits, scaled for resolution. Apply Image to the high layer: 8-bit = Subtract, Scale 2, Offset 128; 16-bit = Add, Scale 2, Offset 0, Invert checked. Set the high layer to Linear Light. On the low layer, use Lasso with a 25–35 px feather plus Gaussian Blur for blotchiness and redness. On the high layer, use Clone Stamp with "Sample must be set to Current Layer". Lower the group opacity to blend back toward the original. — [PHLEARN](https://phlearn.com/tutorial/the-amazing-power-of-frequency-separation-retouching-in-photoshop/)
- Hair styling: Liquify Forward Warp in Advanced Mode, Pressure ≈ 25, Density ≈ 25, "small movements, working from the top down". Clone Stamp: Sample "Current & Below", hardness ≈ 85% ("A hard-edge brush creates a much cleaner, more precise clone stamp result"). Darken mode tones down light flyaways without touching darker areas. Lighten mode adds highlight detail into gaps, Darken adds shadow detail. For transplanted hair: black mask, paint white, and unlink the mask (chain icon) to reposition. — [PHLEARN](https://phlearn.com/tutorial/how-to-style-hair-in-photoshop/)
- Editorial headshot: build a custom textured brush (800×800 doc, natural brush with Shape Dynamics and Scattering) so smoothing isn't plastic. Smooth on a new layer at "flow to around 20–30%", Alt-sampling clean skin. Rebuild detail with a "clipped, desaturated, Overlay-blended High Pass layer". Clone Stamp in Darken to fill hair, so "it only adds darker tone". — [PHLEARN](https://phlearn.com/tutorial/editorial-headshot-retouching-part-1-3/)

### Inferences
- Rule for Clone Stamp blend modes on stray hairs: Darken removes hairs lighter than their surroundings (e.g., blonde or highlighted strands over dark hair or background). Lighten removes hairs darker than their surroundings (dark strands over a light background). This follows from how the modes work: Darken only writes where the source is darker. Some secondary snippets state it backwards.

### Gaps
- PHLEARN pages may have been rewritten since the original videos (text style looks recent), so the numbers reflect the current page, not necessarily the original video.

## Q7. Calvin Hollywood: free technique details

### Takeaway
Calvin Hollywood's best-known free technique is "Freaky Details", documented by third parties: invert a copy, Surface Blur, Vivid Light, stamp visible, set to Overlay. A related skin "glow" variant uses High Pass and Gaussian Blur with Blend If. In interviews he says D&B is his favorite task and Selective Color his favorite adjustment layer.

### Cited Findings
- Freaky Details: duplicate the base → invert → Surface Blur (in his Photoshop original) → blend mode Vivid Light → new layer from visible → set it to Overlay and hide the temporary layer. Surface Blur is used instead of Gaussian to reduce halos at high-contrast edges. The GIMP port uses Gaussian blur radius "around 20%–30% of an image dimension" or G'MIC bilateral smoothing (spatial 25, value 20). — [PIXLS.US](https://pixls.us/articles/freaky-details-calvin-hollywood/); [Pat David](https://patdavid.net/2013/02/calvin-hollywood-freaky-details-in-gimp/)
- "10-step skin retouching (Calvin Hollywood style)", a third-party blog crediting his training: (1) duplicate, Vivid Light, invert; (2) Smart Object, High Pass ≈ 25 px; (3) Gaussian Blur 1–6 px (5 used) to bring detail back; (4) Blend If sliders, Alt-split, to recover highlights and shadows; (5) black mask; (6) paint white, 0% hardness, 40% opacity on face, 80% on neck and body; (7) stamp visible, High Pass < 2 px (1.5), Add Noise 2–3%, Gaussian 0.2 px; (8) Soft Light (or Hard Light/Overlay); (9) opacity 70–90%; (10) group both layers, group opacity 70–90%. — [amisadventureinphotography (secondary)](https://amisadventureinphotography.wordpress.com/2012/08/01/10-step-skin-retouching-calvin-hollywood-style/)
- His quotes: "Dodge and burn is what I really enjoy doing and my favourite adjustment layer is definitely selective color." / "What I really love to do is to use the tools for the things that the tool wasn't designed for." / "The basics are very, very important... the freakiest techniques are all done with basic tools." / "I love edgy, high contrast images with many details." — [Fstoppers interview](https://fstoppers.com/interview/interviews-calvin-hollywood-conceptual-photographer-and-photoshop-master-4015)

### Inferences
- The inverted, blurred Vivid Light copy acts as a high-pass filter with edge-aware (Surface Blur) or plain (Gaussian) blur. Overlaying the stamped result strengthens local contrast. The skin variant reverses the logic: inverted High Pass plus a small blur, masked, smooths mid-frequencies while keeping fine texture. This is close to "invisible blur" or "inverted high-pass" skin smoothing.

### Gaps
- His original Photoshop Surface Blur Radius and Threshold values were not found in free sources. The 10-step recipe comes from a third party, not from Hollywood directly.

## Q8. Other recognized retouchers and signature tricks (D&B variants, helper layers, eye and skin fixes)

### Takeaway
Free Fstoppers and Retouching Academy articles give the most exact, reproducible numbers for D&B variants. Lance Nicoll's "negative D&B" uses 2–3% flow. Julia Kuzmenko McKim's micro D&B uses 1–3% flow at 0–30% hardness and 60–200% zoom. Dani Diamond uses Soft Light 50% gray at 5% and an eye High Pass of 30 in Soft Light. Lulie Talmor recommends a 50%-gray Color-mode layer as the most accurate B&W helper. Dylan Reece uses Channel Mixer monochrome (Red 120–150) as a blemish helper.

### Cited Findings
- **Julia Kuzmenko McKim (Retouching Academy founder): D&B curves.** "Pull one Curve slightly up (brighten in midtones), name this layer 'Dodge' and invert the layer mask color to black"; the Burn layer is the reverse. Don't pull too far, or "even with the lowest brush Opacity/Flow your brush strokes will be too intense". Working opacity 3–5%. Helper: "a 50% gray layer set to Color Blending mode on top of your D&B Curves". Local D&B in "tiny dots and lines" at 60–200% zoom; global D&B with large soft strokes. Then clip Hue/Saturation and Selective Color corrections to the D&B Curves "this way only the areas you have worked on will be affected". — [Fstoppers Ultimate Guide Pt 3](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/ultimate-guide-dodge-burn-technique-part-3-curves-setup-more-9281)
- **Julia Kuzmenko McKim: micro transitions.** Helpers: a darkening, contrast-adding Curves layer plus a 50% gray Color-mode layer. Brush: soft white, hardness 0–30%, opacity 100%, flow 1–3%, size "slightly smaller than the blemish". Change zoom regularly. Mainly dodge the darker micro-transitions and burn lighter spots second. — [Retouching Academy](https://retouchingacademy.com/dodge-burn-working-with-micro-transitions/)
- **Lance Nicoll: "negative dodge and burn".** "the process of minimizing distracting highlights and shadows and also smoothing out the transitional values". Two Curves layers (dodge and burn) with fully black masks. Flow 2–3%, opacity 100%, hardness 0–10%. "continually undo and redo to verify that what you are doing is in fact removing a highlight or shadow instead of creating one". Start new Curves layers as you progress. — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/commercial/dramatic-beauty-tutorial-part-4-what-negative-dodge-and-burn-73214)
- **Dani Diamond: workflow.** Camera Raw: white balance, recover highlights, no global exposure or contrast, brighten skin with the Orange/Red luminance sliders, Clarity ≈ +10. Then spot healing → frequency separation for mismatched tones and under-eye bags → D&B on a Soft Light layer filled with 50% gray, white or black brush at 5% opacity, placed like a makeup artist would → eyes: duplicate, High Pass 30, Soft Light, black mask, paint white at 50% on the eyes only → toning with complementary colors → resize to 2100 px long edge. Quote: "Editing an image will only take a great photo over the edge. It will not change a bad photo into an excellent photo." — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/bts/complete-guide-retouching-portraits-photoshop-free-98236)
- **Dani Diamond: under-eye bags and mismatched tones via Curves eyedropper.** Make a Curves layer, use the top (white-point) eyedropper and click the target color on the cheek, invert the mask, and paint white with a soft, low-flow brush over the dark area. This keeps texture, unlike clone or patch. The steps come from a commenter describing the video. — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/simple-method-fix-bags-under-eyes-and-mismatched-skin-tones-7977)
- **Lulie Talmor: B&W helper choice.** Hue/Saturation −100 is "more muted and with less contrast". A Black & White adjustment layer distorts luminance and leads to over-correction. Recommended: a 50%-gray (or any neutral) Solid Color layer in Color mode, which "best represents the luminosity of each color that the human eye perceives". Optionally add an S-curve above it for contrast. — [Retouching Academy](https://retouchingacademy.com/color-correction-lesson-preview-black-and-white-conversions-in-relation-to-dodging-and-burning/)
- **Dylan Reece: visual aids.** Channel Mixer set to Monochrome with Red ≈ 120 (range 120–150) to find blemishes and guide D&B. Multiple solar curves. B&W in Color mode plus another in Luminosity mode (or a Solid Color in Saturation mode). An Invert layer. A combined invert + B&W + solar "B&W inverted solar curve". Separate saturation, color and luminosity check layers. — [Fstoppers group post](https://fstoppers.com/groups/24744/retouching/110930/visual-aids-help-me-when-im-retouching)
- **Roy Furchgott (Fstoppers, summarizing pro practice):** Curves dodge and burn pair with black masks. Soft white brush at 100% opacity, flow 2% dropping to 1% for fine work, 3–5 px for detail. Start with the burn layer on highlights. Reduce the difference between darks and lights rather than removing it. Expect 2+ hours per image. Quotes Katrin Eismann: "We try to sneak up on the image, like a little bunny rabbit." — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/retouch-skin-top-pro-and-get-paid-one-694381)
- **Clinton Lofthouse: stylized D&B.** Curves Dodge (center pulled up about 1.5 grid squares) and Burn (down about 1.5 squares), masks inverted. Work one region at a time ("I will dodge and burn the face, and nothing else until I finish that area"). How far you push it sets the style. — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/education/using-dodge-and-burn-stylize-your-images-410419)
- **Sean Archer (Stanislav Puchkovsky): philosophy.** "Of course I do retouching, I remove spots and any issues in skin tones. It's very important to keep skin texture, but making it clear and bright. Keeping it natural is the key. I don't like the 'fashion' style retouch, that makes skin lifeless, like it was processed by sandblasting machine… In portraits you have to be an artist, too." Uses texture overlays for backgrounds. — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/stanislav-puchkovsky-aka-sean-archer-master-natural-light-portraits-9584)

### Inferences
- The free sources agree on a D&B recipe an agent can replicate:
  1. Two Curves layers with gentle midtone moves: about ±20 levels (Woloszynowicz 124↔146), "slightly" (McKim), or about 1.5 grid squares for stylized work (Lofthouse).
  2. Black masks.
  3. A soft brush at 100% opacity and 1–5% flow.
  4. A helper group of a 50%-gray Color-mode layer plus a contrast or solar curve.
  5. Corrective or "negative" D&B (micro, then macro) before contouring D&B, as Woloszynowicz, Nicoll and Valentine (large → medium → small passes) all describe.
  6. Color correction (Hue/Saturation, Selective Color) clipped to the D&B layers or masked locally.
  7. Skin hue last (Valentine).
- Valentine's three helper families (Solar, Mid Peak, 50%-gray Color/Negative) cover everything the other authors use. Talmor's comparison supports defaulting to a 50%-gray Color-mode layer rather than Black & White.
